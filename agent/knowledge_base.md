# Ambulance Helpline demo line: knowledge base

## What the helpline does
The Ambulance Helpline is a medical emergency helpline. A caller reaches a call taker, who finds the nearest suitable ambulance and hospital and arranges the booking. The helpline works with partner ambulance operators. It does not run a hospital.

This line is a prototype for demonstration. It does not dispatch ambulances. In a real emergency in India, call 112.

## Where it works
Delhi NCR (Delhi, Gurugram, Noida) and Bangalore. Calls from other cities cannot be served. Those callers should call 112.

## Hours
24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

## Languages
Hindi and English. Callers who speak another language, including Kannada, are passed to a human call taker who calls them back.

## Ambulance types
- **ACLS, advanced life support.** For patients who are critical or may get worse on the way. Carries cardiac monitoring and resuscitation equipment.
- **BLS, basic life support.** For patients who are stable but need oxygen and a trained attendant.
- **PTA, patient transport.** For stable patients who only need to be moved, such as a planned transfer.

The call taker chooses the type from the caller's answers. Callers do not need to know which one they need. If the right type is not available nearby, the call taker offers the nearest basic ambulance instead and says so.

## Cost
- The fare depends on the ambulance type and the distance.
- The call taker states a fare range before booking. The caller decides after hearing it.
- Fares are set by the ambulance partner. The call taker cannot negotiate them. A dispatcher can discuss the fare on the callback.
- Payment is made to the ambulance partner at the end of the trip.

## Transfers
- The helpline arranges transfers from one hospital to another within the city, for stable and unstable patients.
- It does not arrange inter-city transfers.

## What the caller will be asked
1. Location and nearest landmark. The caller may be asked to spell a name.
2. What happened, whether the patient is awake, and whether the patient is breathing normally.
3. Whether another ambulance has already been called.
4. After booking, the helpline confirms with the ambulance operator and calls the caller back. On that call it asks for the patient's name and age and the caller's name.

## What the line cannot do
- It cannot give medical advice, suggest medicines or diagnose.
- It cannot guarantee an arrival time. Times are estimates.
- It cannot help with police or fire emergencies. Call 112.

## If the hospital is closer than the ambulance
For serious cases, if the nearest suitable hospital is at least five minutes closer than the nearest ambulance, the call taker will say so. Going by cab or own vehicle may be faster. The caller chooses.
